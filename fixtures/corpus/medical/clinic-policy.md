# Northwind Medical Group: synthetic clinic policy
Synthetic test document. Fictional clinic, fictional names and figures. Not medical or legal advice.

## 1. Clinic hours and access
1.1. What are the clinic's weekday hours? Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.
1.2. What are the Saturday hours? Saturday, 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.
1.3. What number does a patient call after hours? The after-hours line at 555-0100.
1.4. When is the clinic closed? Sundays and the six observed holidays listed in the policy.
1.5. What is the walk-in policy for urgent problems? Walk-ins are triaged between 8:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m. on weekdays.
1.6. How early should a new patient arrive? Fifteen minutes before the appointment.

## 2. Appointments and cancellations
2.1. How long is a new-patient visit? Sixty minutes.
2.2. How long is a follow-up visit? Twenty minutes.
2.3. How much notice is needed to cancel without a fee? Twenty-four hours.
2.4. What is the no-show fee? Fifty USD.
2.5. How does a patient request an interpreter? Ask scheduling at least two business days before the visit.
2.6. Can a patient request a same-day visit? Yes, through the triage line; same-day slots are released at 7:30 a.m.

## 3. Insurance and authorization
3.1. Which insurance plans are accepted? The plans listed in the accepted-plan table, including the two Medicaid managed-care plans.
3.2. When is eligibility checked? Two business days before the visit and again at check-in.
3.3. How long does a prior authorization take? Five business days after the clinical information is complete.
3.4. Does the clinic accept self-pay patients? Yes, with a 20 percent prompt-pay discount.
3.5. How is secondary insurance handled? The secondary plan is billed after the primary remittance posts.
3.6. What happens when a referral is required but missing? The visit is scheduled but the referral must arrive before the encounter.

## 4. Billing and payments
4.1. When is payment due? At the time of service, after insurance is verified.
4.2. When is the first statement sent? Thirty days after the date of service.
4.3. What is the payment-plan threshold? Balances over five hundred USD can use a six-month plan.
4.4. What is the fee for CPT 99213? One hundred twenty USD.
4.5. What is the fee for CPT 99214? One hundred eighty USD.
4.6. How long does a refund take? Ten business days after approval.

## 5. Medical records
5.1. How long are adult records kept? Seven years after the last visit.
5.2. How long are minor records kept? Until the patient turns twenty-one, or seven years, whichever is longer.
5.3. How long does a records release take? Five business days after a valid authorization.
5.4. How does a patient request an amendment? In writing; the clinic responds within thirty days.
5.5. Can a patient see portal results immediately? Yes, except results the ordering clinician has flagged for a discussion.
5.6. What is the record-copy fee? The fee stated in the state schedule, with the first twenty pages free.

## 6. Privacy and disclosures
6.1. What is the minimum-necessary rule? Use or disclose only the information needed for the purpose.
6.2. When is a separate authorization required? For psychotherapy notes and marketing uses.
6.3. Within what period is a breach notified? Within sixty days of discovery.
6.4. Can a family member receive information? Only with the patient's authorization or when the patient is present and does not object.
6.5. Are faxes allowed? Only to a verified number with a cover sheet.
6.6. Who is the privacy officer? The compliance manager listed in the privacy policy.

## 7. Prescriptions and refills
7.1. How long does a routine refill take? Two business days.
7.2. How often are controlled prescriptions reviewed? Every thirty days; early fills require a clinician review.
7.3. Can a prescription be sent to any pharmacy? Yes, to a pharmacy the patient names and the e-prescribing system supports.
7.4. What happens when a medication is lost? The clinician decides whether a replacement is clinically appropriate; a police report is not required.
7.5. Is an antibiotic given for a viral cold? No; antibiotics are not prescribed for viral upper respiratory infections.
7.6. How are prescription errors reported? To the clinical lead and through the incident system within one business day.

## 8. Referrals and care coordination
8.1. How fast is a routine referral sent? Within three business days.
8.2. Who tracks an urgent referral? The referral coordinator, the same day.
8.3. Does a patient need a referral for a specialist? Only when the plan requires one; the policy lists the exceptions.
8.4. How is an external specialist report filed? Into the patient's chart within two business days of receipt.
8.5. What happens when a referral is denied? The clinician reviews alternatives and the patient is told the reason.
8.6. Can a patient self-refer? Yes, unless the plan requires a primary-care referral.

## 9. Telehealth
9.1. Which platform is used for telehealth? The clinic's video platform, reached from the patient portal.
9.2. What consent is required before a telehealth visit? Verbal consent is documented at the start of the visit.
9.3. Can a controlled substance be prescribed by telehealth? Only when the clinician and the jurisdiction permit it; the policy requires an in-person evaluation first.
9.4. What should a patient do if video fails? Call the clinic and switch to telephone; the visit is documented as telephone.
9.5. Is a telehealth visit billed differently? The same CPT code is used when the service meets the same criteria.
9.6. Where is the telehealth record kept? In the same chart as an in-person visit.

## 10. Emergencies and safety
10.1. What should a patient do for chest pain? Call 911; do not drive to the clinic.
10.2. Where is the emergency kit? At the nurses' station and in each procedure room.
10.3. Who checks the emergency kit? The clinical lead monthly, with a signed log.
10.4. When is an incident report filed? The same business day as the event.
10.5. What is the anaphylaxis first step? Give epinephrine per the standing order and call 911.
10.6. When is the clinic closed for a safety event? When the clinical lead determines the environment is unsafe.
