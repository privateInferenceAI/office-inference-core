# Attack: client reality

Verdict: yes with changes.

I read the brief, the six reports, manual sections 13a to 13c and 13j to 13m, the requirements, the architecture, the operator guide and the code the brief names. I changed no code.

I judged the plan against the standard section 13j sets for an approval: the record is exactly as strong as the evidence behind it. A citation is the other artifact a client reads and believes without checking. The direction is right. The answer path needs a cap, the chunker needs a post-condition, and the verifier must read what the model saw. The plan is not deliverable as written. The office lane is sized to a placeholder, and the client never sees the truncation. The migration is a destructive window with no rollback. Eleven changes would have to land before a client deployment.

## Attacks that would change the plan

Ranked by harm to a client or an operator. The first three are the ones I would not ship past.

**The client's experience, in one paragraph.** On the frontier lane the result is better than a naive search for the questions a small business asks. Hybrid retrieval finds the identifiers, the page citation opens, and the verifier marks a substituted digit. The office lane is the problem. Three excerpts at 1,600 bytes come to about 943 tokens of prose, less than half a page, and the model is told to stay inside the excerpt. A single-fact question is answerable. A policy question that runs two paragraphs is not. A table row after the cut is not, and neither is a question that needs two documents. The answer will say so in a way that sounds like the document is silent. The plan does not answer the naive-search question for that lane. It answers a narrower question and calls it the answer.

### 1. The office lane is sized to a placeholder and cuts most prose passages below the chunker's own floor

The plan gives the office lane three passages at 1,600 bytes each (brief section 3a, `answer-context-budget.md:207-219`). The chunker's floor is 384 tokens and its ceiling 512 (`services/ingestion/chunker.py:46-47`). At the brief's own measured prose ratio of 5.09 bytes per token, a 384-token chunk is about 1,954 bytes and a 512-token chunk is about 2,606. The 1,600-byte cap is below the smallest full prose chunk the system aims to produce. Most full-page prose passages on the office lane are excerpts, not passages. The client gets a partial source on ordinary questions, not just on the rare oversized table.

The window has room for more. Three passages at 1,600 bytes is 4,800 bytes, about 943 tokens at prose density and about 3,609 at the dense-table worst case. Add 700 output tokens and the 512 reserve and the prompt is roughly 1,400 to 5,700 tokens against an 8,192 window. The evidence report's own arithmetic says six 512-token chunks is about 3,072 tokens, which fits the 8,192 window with room for output (`chunking-strategy-evidence.md:119`). The plan cuts the office client from up to about 3,072 tokens of context to about 943, a 69 percent reduction, on a window with room for the old amount. The report's prose rejects the number the table adopts: "I would not lower it to three for every build just because the office lane has an 8K window" (`answer-context-budget.md:43`).

The cap is calibrated to a placeholder. The renderer's default local model is `Qwen3-1.7B-Q4_K_M.gguf` at `MODEL_CTX` 8,192 (`scripts/render_manifest.py:44,334-335`). The in-office reference hardware is a GPU box running a 14B or 32B model at 32K. A client who buys that box and sets `MODEL_CTX=32768` still gets three 1,600-byte excerpts, because the plan never reads the deployment's window. The report says the number "must be read from the deployment rather than assumed from the model's data sheet" (`answer-context-budget.md:224`). The plan does not read it.

**Scenario.** A 12-person firm asks "what does our lease say about renewal and the notice period?" The renewal clause starts 1,100 bytes into a 2,400-byte chunk. The office cap takes 1,600 bytes, and the notice period sits after the cut. The model is told the block is an excerpt and must not describe anything outside it. It answers that the excerpt does not state the notice period. The document does. The client opens the citation, sees the full chunk with the notice period in it, and concludes the assistant is broken. Ctrl-F would have found it.

**Change required.** Derive the passage count and per-passage cap from `MODEL_CTX` and the lane, not from a fixed table. Keep six passages with a total byte cap and a per-content-class estimate, so prose gets the room it needs and dense tables drop passages from the bottom. Measure three against six on the question set before shipping the office numbers. A 512-token chunk at 5.09 bytes per token is not a table, and the budget should not treat it as one.

### 2. The migration command in the brief is the one that caused the 171-document incident

The brief says "single-writer at `INGEST_CONCURRENCY=1` (see `RET-INGEST-002`)" (brief, section 3c). `RET-INGEST-002` is open. Its note records that a manual `docker exec ... ingest.py` ran beside the 15-minute service cycle, both workers shared one embedding service, TEI answered 429, and 171 documents failed a pass with some dead-lettered (`docs/requirements.yaml:708-727`). The wanted fix, which the note says is not made, is to use `oi restart ingestion`, which runs the loop's first cycle and stays the only writer. `INGEST_CONCURRENCY=1` is not in the compose environment. The code default is four (`services/ingestion/ingest.py:67`), so the operator has to pass it with `-e` or edit the compose file. The brief names neither.

The manual's documented way to re-ingest is `docker exec ingestion python3 /app/ingest.py --documents /documents` (`docs/manual.md:2085`). That is exactly the command that creates the second worker. The brief cites the requirement for its concurrency number and does not apply the requirement's actual finding.

**Scenario.** The operator opens a terminal at 18:00, passes `-e INGEST_CONCURRENCY=1` to a one-off `docker exec`, and starts. At 18:15 the container's loop starts its own cycle. Two writers, one TEI. Some documents fail, a few reach the dead-letter latch. The migration prints a failure count at the end and exits non-zero. The operator sees a wall of JSON, the client's search is missing documents, and the per-document progress lines are buried above the summary.

**Change required.** Stop the loop or use `oi restart ingestion`, set concurrency 1 in the compose environment, and put the exact command in the brief with a preflight that proves no second worker is running. The same preflight should check that `DATABASE_URL` is set, because an in-memory manifest re-ingests everything and says so in one line (`docs/manual.md:2206-2208`).

### 3. A failed document is deleted before it is replaced, and the dead-letter latch hides it

`write_document` deletes every chunk of the document first, then writes the new points (`services/ingestion/ingest.py:1459`). If the parse or embed fails after the delete, the document is gone from the index. `record_failure` writes the failure against the manifest, and after two permanent failures the document is dead-lettered and not retried until its bytes change. The container's healthcheck runs `--status`, which reads the last completed cycle. A dead-lettered document drops out of the next cycle, so the container reports healthy. The manual says this plainly at `docs/manual.md:1993` and `docs/manual.md:2119`: healthy is not evidence that a corpus loaded, and `--failures` is the only command that remembers.

The plan's post-migration list has `--scan-duplicates` and counts. It does not have `--failures`, a per-document diff, or `--clear-failures`. Clearing is required after any code fix because the latch is keyed on the content hash (`docs/manual.md:2140`). A v3 chunker bug that fails a class of documents dead-letters them all, and fixing the code does not change the bytes, so the fix looks like it did not work.

**Scenario.** A 40-person firm has 900 documents, 30 of them scanned supplier contracts. The v3 table split handles most of them but fails on a two-column scan. That contract was searchable under v2. After the migration it is not. The total chunk count is within 5 percent of the before count, so the operator signs off. The contract is missing until somebody asks for it and gets "No document you may read answers this question."

**Change required.** Take a `--failures` baseline before the window. Run `--failures` after it, and diff per-document chunk counts, not totals. Run `--clear-failures` after any code fix. Better, keep the old generation until the new one is verified, which is attack 4.

### 4. There is no snapshot, no progress query, and no rollback. "Record the counts" is a confession, not a mitigation

The operator guide's stage 12 says the backup feature does not exist: no backup script, no `services/backup/`, no restore procedure, `OPS-BACKUP-001` open (`docs/operator-guide.md:1610-1617`). The plan's migration is two to four and a half hours of destructive rewrite. The brief's mitigation is "record the migration plan and the counts before and after" (brief, section 3c). That helps the postmortem. It does not help at hour two, and it does not restore anything.

What the operator sees at hour two, if the process is interrupted by an SSH drop, an OOM, or a 429 storm: the per-document lines stop, the cycle summary never prints, and `--status` shows the last completed cycle rather than the interrupted one. The index is mixed. Documents already processed have v3 chunk ids, the rest have v2. The collection contract says v3. One document may be mid-rewrite and absent, because delete comes first. The last document's lexical rows may be old. There is no command that counts chunks by `chunker_version`. The manifest has no `chunker_version` column (`db/migrations/007_ingest_manifest.sql`), so the worker has no per-document generation field. Only `chunk_index` and the Qdrant payload carry it, and no product command reads them that way. The brief proposes adding the manifest key afterwards, which is a fourth schema change after the window, not a progress tool during it.

The brief says "no blue/green path" because there is one collection and `chunk_index` has no collection column. That is true for a second collection. It is not true for a second generation. `chunk_index` already has a `chunker_version` column (`db/migrations/010_chunk_index.sql:34`) and the Qdrant payload already carries it (`services/ingestion/chunker.py:113`). A read-side pin on the generation and a delete that keeps the old version would let the old index serve while the new one builds. The oversized report's own repair step says "keep the old points until the new ones pass" and "delete old points by `(doc_id, chunker_version < new)` after verification" (`oversized-elements-and-repair.md:159`). The brief adopts the fatalism and not the alternative. The client pays for that choice with a four-hour outage and no rollback.

**Scenario.** At 20:10 the operator's laptop sleeps. The `docker exec` dies mid-document. Staff are still working. Search returns a mix of new and old chunks, and one contract is missing. The operator restarts, the worker re-derives the documents still on v5, and the window doubles. The client asks "is it done?" and the honest answer is "I don't know how far it got."

**Change required.** Before the window, take a Qdrant collection snapshot and a `pg_dump` of `chunk_index` and `ingest_manifest`. Manual snapshots are possible even though the backup feature is not built. During the window, a `select chunker_version, count(*) from chunk_index group by 1` progress query, or a `--versions` command. A read-side generation pin, or an explicit statement that this is a four-hour outage with no rollback and the client has agreed to it. The current text reads as if recording the plan were the mitigation. It is not.

### 5. The read path is not protected, and the contract bump can refuse every search or be bypassed by the cache

The brief says the chunker version is in the embedding contract, so a mismatched contract stops the worker (brief, section 3c). It does not say what the reader does. `retriever.verify_contract` calls `contract.check` without `allow_change` (`services/retrieval/retriever.py:251`), and the live contract includes `chunker.CHUNKER_VERSION` (`services/ingestion/contract.py:121`). Deploy an API image that declares v3 while the recorded contract is v2, and every search raises. The check is cached only after success (`services/retrieval/retriever.py:213-255`), so the refusal repeats on every question until the writer records the new generation. If the API restarts after the writer records v3, the first search passes. The order matters and the brief does not state it.

The other direction is worse. If the API process is not restarted, its `_CONTRACT_CHECKED` set already contains the collection from a v2 search, so it skips the check and keeps serving v2 code against a v3 contract. The contract's own docstring says a reader cannot get an answer built on vectors from another contract. During the migration it can, because the cache assumes the process is restarted when the contract changes.

**Scenario.** The operator deploys the new ingestion image but leaves the API on the old image to avoid downtime. The writer updates the contract to v3 at the first cycle. The old API keeps answering with the old chunking and without the answer-path cap, which is the state the client was in before the fix. The operator believes the migration is live. It is not.

**Change required.** State the restart order. Make the reader's contract check include a generation or a short TTL, so a mid-run change is noticed. Add a preflight that proves the API and the writer agree before the window, and a post-check that names the contract generation the API is serving.

### 6. The client never sees the truncation, and the citation panel's promise breaks

The operator guide says the citation chip opens "the text the model answered from," not the file on disk (`docs/operator-guide.md:2213`). Today that is the full chunk. After the plan trims the passage, the chip either opens the full chunk, which is text the model did not see, or it opens the excerpt, which the app does not have. The answer payload's citations carry `index`, `doc_id`, `acl`, `page_start` and `page_end` (`services/api/answer.py:271`). No bytes, no excerpt marker, no truncation flag. The plan says it discloses truncation and puts the disclosure in the audit record. The person reading the answer is not the auditor.

The verifier change makes this worse before it makes it better. On the office lane most passages are excerpts, so a claim whose number sits in the tail becomes `outside_included_excerpt`. The client sees `unsupported` beside a figure that is in the document the chip opens. The manual's own rule is that a check which refuses correct answers gets turned off (`docs/manual.md:2621`). The plan does not measure the rate.

**Scenario.** A 6-person firm asks for the standard mileage rate. The rate is early in the fee schedule, so the answer is fine. The next question asks for the after-hours rate, which sits at byte 1,900 of the passage. The model says the excerpt does not state it. The chip opens the full schedule and shows it. The client stops trusting the "unsupported" mark, and then the check is decoration.

**Change required.** The citation object carries the included range and a truncation flag. The app opens the excerpt with a one-line banner: "Excerpt shown to the model: 1,600 of 2,406 bytes." The answer carries a visible note when any cited passage is an excerpt. The verifier distinguishes "not in the source" from "not in the excerpt." This is a client-facing change, not an audit-only one. The app is a separate checkout and the API contract changes, so it belongs in the plan rather than in a follow-up.

### 7. The audit record cannot prove what the model saw, and the plan says it can

The plan records context bytes and hashes, not text. That is defensible for a tamper-evident chain, but it is not evidence of what the model saw. The hash has no preimage in the record. To recompute it, an auditor needs the chunk text, the chunker version, the parser version and the byte offsets into the extracted text. The record has `context_version` but not the chunk id or the parser digest. The chunk id is not in the citation. The chunk payload has no byte offset (`services/ingestion/chunker.py:107-134`). After the re-chunk, the old chunk text is gone. The hash proves the row was not altered. It does not prove the excerpt matched it.

The field name makes it worse. `prompt_sha256` today is the hash of the redacted question (`services/api/answer.py:290-296`), not the assembled prompt. Add `prompt_bytes` for the assembled prompt and an auditor will read the pair as a hash and a size of the same thing. They are not.

The blanket "no text in the record" also overrides the deployment's content policy. The stack already has `CONTENT_MODE_*` for `hash`, `redacted` and `full` per data class. A client whose public documents are `full` could have the excerpt in the record. The plan forbids it for every client. That is the client's decision, not the brief's.

**Scenario.** Six months after an answer, the client's auditor asks whether the model saw the paragraph that supports a cited figure. The chunk was re-chunked in the migration. The excerpt hash cannot be recomputed. The record shows a hash and a byte count, and the answer shows `supported: true`. The auditor can prove the row is intact and cannot prove the model saw the paragraph. The honest sentence is weaker than the one the plan implies.

**Change required.** Record the chunk id, the chunker version, the parser digest and the offsets into the extracted text, or store the excerpt under the content mode. Rename `prompt_sha256` or add `question_sha256` and a real prompt hash. Version the verifier's rules the way `TAXONOMY_VERSION` versions the failure classification, and record that version on every answer. State the bound in the manual: the record is tamper-evident, and it is reproducible only while the source and the extractor are retained.

### 8. The office question cap will refuse the questions a small business actually asks

The plan sets an 800-byte question cap on the office lane and says to refuse a longer question with a typed 400 rather than truncate it (`answer-context-budget.md:274`). The current `MAX_QUESTION_CHARS` is 32,000 (`services/api/app.py:110`). That is a 40 times reduction. Eight hundred bytes is about 130 words. A client pasting a lease clause and asking "does this allow subletting?" is over the cap. The refusal is correct in principle, because truncating the question changes what was asked. The number is chosen to fit the budget, not to fit a person. The office window has room for a 2,000-byte question with three passages, or a 1,200-byte question with four. The plan protects the passages and refuses the intent.

The cap is not documented. `OI_MAX_QUESTION_CHARS` appears in the code and not in the manual's environment table, so the client's IT cannot find it.

**Scenario.** A 20-person firm's office manager pastes a 300-word clause from a supplier contract and asks whether it permits an assignment. The API returns a 400. The app may show a generic error, which I could not verify, so the client sees a failure rather than "shorten the question." The client paraphrases, loses the clause, and gets an answer about the wrong thing.

**Change required.** Raise the office question cap to a number a person can use, and make the refusal message say what to do. Measure the cap against real questions, not the window arithmetic. Document the knob.

### 9. The new refusal reason has no client-facing definition, and the plan does not measure how often it fires

This is the mechanism behind attacks 1 and 6, and it deserves its own number because it is the part that changes the client's trust. `verify_claim` builds its haystack from `passage.text` (`services/retrieval/citations.py:267`). The plan changes it to the included excerpt and adds `outside_included_excerpt`. The check is right in the narrow sense: a claim whose supporting words are outside what the model saw should not be certified. The client will not read it that way. On the office lane most passages are excerpts, so the reason will be common, and the client will see `unsupported` beside a figure that is in the document the chip opens. The plan does not say how the reason is rendered, how often it fires, or how the client tells it apart from a model that invented a number. The manual's own rule is that a check which refuses correct answers gets turned off (`docs/manual.md:2621`). A mark that is technically correct but reads as a false accusation has the same effect.

**Scenario.** A 6-person firm asks for the after-hours rate. The rate is in the document at byte 1,900 of the passage. A 32B local model that has seen this trade's common rates states a plausible figure from prior knowledge. The verifier refuses it as `outside_included_excerpt`, which is the correct verdict for the evidence. The client opens the citation, sees the figure, and reads "unsupported" as "the system says this is wrong." The client cannot tell that the refusal is about the excerpt, not the source, so the next wrong answer gets the same label and the mark loses its meaning.

**Change required.** Before enabling the refusal, measure how often `outside_included_excerpt` fires on the corpus and the question set. Render it as "outside the excerpt the model was shown" with a link to the full source, not as "unsupported." For the office lane, do not truncate prose passages, or raise the cap so the common case is a full passage. Publish the measured rate beside the figure checks' rate, and keep the overclaim flag separate from the refusal.

### 10. The budget has no operator knob, and the existing one now misleads

The manual documents `OI_PASSAGES` as "how many passages retrieval asks for" (`docs/manual.md:5530`). After the plan, the byte cap can drop passages, so raising `OI_PASSAGES` may change nothing. The per-passage, total-passage and prompt caps have no environment names and no manifest fields. The question cap has `OI_MAX_QUESTION_CHARS` in the code and not in the manual. The office budget is not derived from `MODEL_CTX`. A client's IT, who is supposed to take the system over, can edit the compose file for the existing knobs but cannot tune the new ones without editing code. The manual's environment table is the contract for what they can change. The most consequential quality knob would be missing from it.

**Scenario.** The client's IT reads that answers are short, finds `OI_PASSAGES=6` in the manual, sets it to 10, restarts the API, and sees the same three passages because the lane cap is a constant. They conclude the knob is broken, or that the vendor shipped a different build. Neither is a support call the client should have to make.

**Change required.** Name the knobs, default them from the lane and `MODEL_CTX`, and add them to the manual's environment table with the same "edit the compose file and restart" note. Make `OI_PASSAGES` honest: if the cap drops a passage, the response says so.

### 11. The time estimate is a dev-corpus number presented as a client window, and the client is told nothing

The brief says two to four and a half hours for 341 documents and 21,000 to 25,000 chunks (brief, section 3c). That is the reference corpus, not a client corpus. A 50-person firm can have thousands of documents and hundreds of scanned pages. The manual says a first run over a client's corpus takes minutes or hours (`docs/manual.md:2000`). The brief gives no documents-per-hour or chunks-per-hour figure, no dry-run estimate, and no measurement on office hardware. The operator will quote three hours and discover twelve.

The plan has no client communication: no maintenance window, no "search is degraded" notice, no ETA, no post-migration confirmation. The operator guide has a model sentence for the backup gap (`docs/operator-guide.md:1653`). The migration has none. At hour two the operator's honest sentence is "I don't know how far it got, search is mixed, and some documents may be missing." That sentence should be prepared before the window, not improvised during it.

**Scenario.** The operator starts at 17:00 on a Friday, tells the client "a couple of hours," and at 22:00 the parse is 60 percent done. The client's weekend staff use the assistant on Saturday. The index is mixed and one document is dead-lettered. The client's first experience of the improved retrieval is a missing answer.

**Change required.** Measure throughput on the reference box and the office box, state a formula and a dry-run estimate, schedule the window out of hours, send a client notice before and after, and give the operator a status sentence with the progress query from attack 4.

## Attacks that are risks to document and accept

- **`coverage_overclaim` will fire on policy prose.** On the office lane the window never covers the whole chunk, so every sentence with "all", "every" or "none" is a candidate, including "all employees must submit a receipt." The research says measure the false-positive rate before turning it into a refusal (`answer-context-budget.md:134`). The brief says "add overclaim flags" without saying flag or refusal. Ship it as a flag, scope it to table and list claims, and measure. An auditor must not read a false flag as "no overclaim."
- **The verifier cannot catch a claim resting on the omitted part in a non-totalizing way.** The research says this (`answer-context-budget.md:163`). The plan should repeat it in the client-facing language, because `supported` will still mean "every mechanical check passed against the excerpt", not "true" and not "the whole document agrees."
- **The office lane's reranker is unresolved.** The report's case for six passages rests on the reranker being the control that makes six good passages possible (`answer-context-budget.md:43`). The office build's reranking is out of scope in the requirements (`RET-RERANK-001`), the compose has a CPU reranker behind a bench profile (`deploy/office/compose/docker-compose.yml:83-96`), and the API defaults to the Bedrock model. If the office build is unreranked, three passages are three guesses. Verify which reranker the office build runs before shipping the three-passage number.
- **The 81 documents with oversized chunks have truncated vectors today.** TEI truncates at 8,192 tokens, so the 439 KB table's vector covers a prefix (`brief, section 1`). The re-chunk fixes that, which is a real client win. Until the re-chunk, retrieval for those documents is unreliable. Quarantine them or warn the operator, and do not promise the migration is only about prompt length.
- **Existing answers and saved citations.** The answer payload stores document, access label and page, not chunk id (`services/api/answer.py:271`), so a page-level citation survives the re-chunk. The verifier's meaning does not. An old `supported: true` was computed against the full chunk, so a re-run under the new verifier could refuse it. Version the verifier and state that old answers keep their original verdict, or the audit replay will disagree with the record.
- **The table-row split changes the citation unit.** A citation becomes a row range with a repeated header, not a whole table. That is the right unit, and the client must see the range and the header, not just a page. Otherwise the citation opens a table and leaves the reader to find the row.
- **The manifest `chunker_version` column is a fourth schema change.** The brief puts it afterwards, as future-proofing. Doing it after the window means backfilling it from the payload, or accepting that the next chunker change still needs a payload bump. Do it in the window, with a migration, or say why not.
- **`EMBED_CONTRACT_ALLOW_CHANGE` must be unset after the run.** A client's IT who leaves it set has a deployment that accepts the next model change silently. The manual documents the variable but not the unset step (`docs/manual.md:5538`). Add it to the migration procedure.
- **The brief's duplicate signature is wrong in detail.** It says the signature is `md5(text)` per chunk (brief, section 3c). The detector uses chunk ids, which are uuid5 over the content hash (`docs/requirements.yaml:755`). The ratios do move and `--scan-duplicates` is the right re-run. A client's IT will search for md5 and find nothing.
- **The frontier cap is far below the 300K window.** That is right for a six-passage answer. The product still needs a separate path for whole-document tasks, and a client sentence for why the 300K model does not get the whole contract. Without it, a client will ask for a summary of a 200-page document and get six passages.

## What the plan gets right for a client

- **The diagnosis is real and the failure is client-visible.** A 439 KB table and a 228,326-token prompt is a failed request, not a slow answer. The cap is necessary. A client who asks a question today can get a provider error. After the cap, they get a short answer or the passages. That is better.
- **The bytes-per-token measurement is the right instrument.** The estimator was about 15 times low on a table. A 512-token ceiling checked with it was never a ceiling. Measuring bytes per token on the actual formats is the only way to know what a prompt costs.
- **The verifier change is the right call.** Verifying against the full chunk after trimming would certify claims the model could not see. That is worse than no check, because the record would say "supported" with confidence.
- **The table policy is right for a client's documents.** Keep a table whole when it fits, split by rows with the header repeated, keep the row range. That is the citation unit a client can open and check. It is also what Docling does, which is the reference the report chose.
- **The three-part version act is correct and non-obvious.** A chunker bump alone does nothing, a payload bump alone re-derives with the old chunking, and the contract stops the worker before it ingests. The brief says this and it is true.
- **The honesty about no blue/green and about the estimator is the right tone.** The brief says what it cannot do. It does not pretend the migration is atomic.
- **The rejection of the fashionable upgrades is right for a 5 to 50 person client.** Semantic chunking, late chunking, RAPTOR and GraphRAG add cost, break labels or break citations. The measured gains do not justify them at this scale.
- **The fix improves the vectors, not just the prompt.** The 81 documents with oversized chunks have truncated embeddings today. The re-chunk makes them retrievable, which is a client win beyond the prompt budget.
- **The degraded answer returns the client's own passages.** That is better than an apology and better than a cheaper model answering under the same branding.

## What I could not verify

- I did not run the system, the migration or the probes. There is no box access from this session. The two to four and a half hour estimate, the 81 of 341 count and the 21,000 to 25,000 chunk count are taken from the brief and the reports.
- I did not read the client app (`oic-ui`). The claim that the chip opens the full chunk is inferred from the API's document endpoint and the operator guide's sentence, not from the app's code. The app's handling of a 400 from `/ask` is also unverified.
- The office build's actual hardware and model. The render default is `Qwen3-1.7B` at 8,192, but a sold box may be a GPU with 32K. I did not find a deployment that fixes the office `MODEL_CTX`.
- The office reranker. The compose has a CPU reranker behind a profile, the API defaults to Bedrock, and the requirements say office reranking is out of scope. I did not verify which the office build runs, so the "unreranked three passages" scenario depends on that answer.
- Whether a manual Qdrant snapshot is part of any existing procedure. Stage 12 says no backup, but the Qdrant API supports snapshots and an operator could take one. I did not test the restore.
