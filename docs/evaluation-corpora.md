# Evaluation corpora

What we may use to build the fixture corpus and the question sets, and what we may not. Verified by
reading licence pages and dataset cards on 2026-10-11; nothing was downloaded.

**Why this document exists.** The first list was written from memory in a conversation, and three of
its recommendations turned out to be unusable: SlideVQA is under an evaluation agreement, the DocVQA
family is account-gated with third-party document provenance, and RVL-CDIP traces back to a tobacco
litigation archive with no clean grant. A fourth, MS MARCO, is non-commercial only, and it also
contaminates one subset of a benchmark we would otherwise have used. That is the argument for
verifying a licence from the source rather than recalling it, which `AGENTS.md` now requires before a
component is chosen.

**Status column:** `VERIFIED-OK` means a company may use it internally for commercial product
development, with no signed agreement, fee or academic-only clause. `PROBLEM` means a restriction that
blocks that use. `UNCLEAR` means no licence statement could be found. `UNVERIFIED` means the claim could
not be confirmed.


Scope: internal engineering evaluation of a self-hosted document-search system for small
businesses. Nothing is redistributed or shipped to customers. Verification method: reading
official licence pages, dataset cards, GitHub LICENSE files and API metadata only. No
datasets were downloaded. Checked 2026-10-11.

**How to read the Licence column.** "Page read" is the exact page the licence statement was
read on. `VERIFIED-OK` means a company can use it internally for commercial product
development with no signed agreement, no fee and no academic-only clause. `PROBLEM` means a
restriction, fee or gating that blocks that use as written. `UNCLEAR` means no licence
statement could be found or the terms are not stated in reusable form. `UNVERIFIED` means I
could not confirm the claim and am not guessing.

## Licence verdict roll-up

**PROBLEM (do not use as-is for commercial internal evaluation):**
- MS MARCO — explicitly non-commercial research only, and it contaminates the `msmarco` config inside RAGBench.
- SlideVQA — NTT "SOFTWARE LICENSE AGREEMENT FOR EVALUATION" plus no licence on the HF mirror.
- DocVQA / MP-DocVQA — RRC login-gated, terms on the download page, images from the UCSF Industry Documents Library.
- RVL-CDIP — IIT-CDIP / Legacy Tobacco Documents Library provenance, no clean grant.
- PubLayNet images (annotations are fine) — PMC Open Access Subset Terms of Use.
- Avocado email collection (missing-list item) — LDC catalogue, licence fee or membership required.

**UNCLEAR (no licence statement found; ask before relying on it):**
- FinanceBench (no LICENSE; full set by email), FUNSD (template licence only), SROIE (registration-gated, no licence), SpreadsheetBench main benchmark (no LICENSE; the Verified HF subset is CC BY-SA 4.0), Enron at CMU (research framing only — use the CC BY 3.0 EDO distribution instead), MAUD, DocILE data (gated), EnronQA (no licence metadata), Kaggle datasets generally.

**Wrong or moved URLs found:**
- RAGBench is `galileo-ai/ragbench`, not `rungalileo/RAGBench`.
- IRS prior-year forms: `https://apps.irs.gov/app/picklist/list/priorFormPublication.html` now 302-redirects to `https://www.irs.gov/prior-year-forms-and-instructions`.
- promptfoo: canonical home is `https://github.com/promptfoo/promptfoo`.
- Papers with Code: site is shut down; `paperswithcode.com/datasets` is gone.
- DocVQA/MP-DocVQA have no open download page; the only route is the RRC portal (`https://rrc.cvc.uab.es/?ch=17` and `?ch=18`).
- (Not a wrong URL but worth knowing: `docile-invoices/docile` and `kensho/tobacco800` on HuggingFace return 401 — gated or not public under those ids.)

| **Enron via EnronData.org** (one thread with attachments, ~10–20 messages) | The email-thread case: quoted replies, attachments, folder structure, threading and sender/recipient metadata. | CC BY 3.0 US (use the EDO distribution, not the CMU copy) |
| **SpreadsheetBench Verified** (3–5 workbooks) | The spreadsheet case: real, messy .xlsx business workbooks with find/extract/sum instructions you can reuse as golden answers. | CC BY-SA 4.0 (Verified subset only) |

Add **CORD** (three receipt scans) if you want an invoice-shaped scanned document with a permissive licence, and **pdf.js test PDFs** as a parser crash-test set that costs nothing.

**The slide-deck gap is real.** The only slide option on the list, SlideVQA, is under an NTT evaluation agreement. Do not build a fixture on it. Author 3–5 slide decks yourself (LibreOffice can export .pptx), include a table on one slide and a chart on another, and export one to PDF. Same argument for a "policy" document: the sample expense-policy doc already in the repo is the right template, and an IRS publication PDF gives you a dense real-world policy with unambiguous pagination.

Why not RVL-CDIP, DocVQA or PubLayNet as the scanned fixture: licence and provenance problems, and none of them look like a small business's paperwork. DocLayNet gets you the pixels without the tobacco-litigation chain.

## 3. The trap with auto-generating golden questions from your own corpus

The trap is that automatically generated questions are answerable **by construction, in the generator's words, against the chunk the generator was shown**. That makes the evaluation measure a different — and much easier — task than the one you care about. The specific biases:

1. **Circularity / retrieval is assumed correct.** Ragas-style pipelines pull a chunk, then ask a model to write a question that chunk answers. If retrieval fetched the wrong chunk, the question is anchored to the wrong source page. Your golden page numbers inherit retrieval's mistakes, and the benchmark cannot detect retrieval failures it created.
2. **Lexical leakage.** The generated question reuses the document's own nouns and phrasing. Both BM25 and embedding models score high when the query and the passage share vocabulary. Real users paraphrase and use their own words ("how long do I have to keep receipts?" versus "retention period"). A generated set systematically overstates recall on exactly the queries your users type.
3. **Easy-sentence bias.** Generators pick sentences that verbalise cleanly: definitions, named entities, single numbers. They under-sample cross-references ("see Section 7.2"), answers that are a cell computed from two table columns, and answers split across two pages. Those are where retrieval actually breaks.
4. **No unanswerable questions.** Nothing in the pipeline generates "this document does not say". So the generated set never measures abstention — the single behaviour that matters most for a system that cites pages, and the reason SQuAD v2's unanswerable half is worth reading.
5. **Answer support is trivially satisfied.** The answer is copied from the context, so any faithfulness metric reports near-perfect scores. You never learn whether the system cites the page a human would cite.
6. **Coverage is chunk-weighted.** Generation runs over chunks, so long documents produce most of the questions and short but important documents (one-page policies, a single spreadsheet) produce almost none. The aggregate score is a document-length weighting you did not choose.
7. **Self-agreement.** If the same model family writes the questions and grades the answers, you are measuring a model's agreement with itself, not correctness.
8. **On-premises leak.** Ragas and DeepEval both assume an LLM. Pointed at a hosted API, your customers' contracts and emails leave the building — the one thing the product promises never happens.

**What to do instead.** Keep generated questions as a smoke test: a cheap tripwire that ingestion, chunking and the vector index still work after a change. Then hand-write the acceptance set, and deliberately write against the biases above:
- paraphrase so the question shares few words with the passage;
- include several questions the corpus cannot answer, and require a refusal;
- require the cited page number in the expected answer, and check it;
- include at least two questions whose answer needs two cells from a table, and one that needs two documents;
- write questions over the small, awkward documents as well as the long ones;
- have someone who has not tuned the retriever write a held-out subset, and do not tune against it.

A generated set tells you whether the plumbing works. It cannot tell you whether search is good, because it was built by the thing you are testing.
